OPTION PRICING MODELS
Black-Scholes Model
The foundational model for pricing European options. Uses 5 inputs: stock price (S), strike price (K), time to expiry (T), risk-free rate (r), and volatility (σ).
C = S·N(d₁) - K·e^(-rT)·N(d₂)
Binomial Model
Prices options using a discrete-time lattice. Supports American options (early exercise). More flexible than Black-Scholes.
Monte Carlo Simulation
Simulates thousands of price paths using random walks. Best for exotic options and path-dependent payoffs.

FOREX CONCEPTS & DEFINITIONS
PIP (Price Interest Point)
FOREX BASICS
The smallest standard price move. For most pairs it's 0.0001 (4th decimal). For JPY pairs it's 0.01 (2nd decimal). A pip move on 1 standard lot = $10.
Spread
TRANSACTION COST
Difference between bid (sell) and ask (buy) price. Major pairs have tighter spreads (1-3 pips). Exotics can have 30-100+ pip spreads. Represents broker profit.
Lot Size
POSITION SIZING
Standard Lot = 100,000 units. Mini Lot = 10,000 units. Micro Lot = 1,000 units. Nano = 100 units. Determines pip value and risk exposure.
Leverage
MARGIN
Control large positions with small capital. 50:1 leverage = control $50,000 with $1,000. Amplifies both profits and losses. US max = 50:1 majors, 20:1 minors.

FUTURES CONCEPTS
Contango
Futures price > spot price. Normal market structure. Indicates storage costs and positive carry. Rolling futures forward costs money.
Futures Price = Spot × e^(r+s)T where s = storage cost
Backwardation
Futures price < spot price. Indicates near-term supply shortage or high convenience yield. Rolling futures forward earns money (positive roll yield).
Roll Yield
Profit/loss from rolling an expiring futures contract to the next month. Negative in contango, positive in backwardation. Critical for commodity ETFs.
Margin & Leverage
Initial margin ~5-15% of contract value. Maintenance margin triggers margin calls. One E-Mini S&P contract = $250 × index. At 5,200 = $1.3M exposure with ~$14K margin.

BOND MATH
Duration (Modified)
Measures price sensitivity to interest rate changes. A bond with duration 7 loses ~7% in price if rates rise 1%.
ΔP/P ≈ -D_mod × Δy
Convexity
Second-order rate sensitivity. Positive convexity = price rises more than it falls for equal rate moves. Always adds value to bonds.
YTM vs Current Yield
Current Yield = Annual Coupon / Price. YTM accounts for price discount/premium amortized to maturity. YTM is the true return if held to maturity.

CRYPTO CONCEPTS
On-Chain Metrics
NVT Ratio (Network Value to Transactions): like P/E for crypto. MVRV (Market Value to Realized Value): over 3.0 = overvalued historically. SOPR tracks profit taking.
Halving
BTC block reward cuts in half every 210,000 blocks (~4 years). Reduces new supply issuance. Historically precedes major bull runs. Next halving: April 2024 (3.125 BTC/block).
Funding Rate
Periodic payment between long and short traders in perpetual futures. Positive = longs pay shorts (bullish crowding). Negative = shorts pay longs. Resets every 8 hours.
Liquidation Cascade
Rapid forced selling of leveraged positions. Creates chain reaction of margin calls. Visible in "liquidation heatmaps" which show clusters of stops. Amplifies volatility.

TECHNICAL INDICATORS MASTER REFERENCE
RSI (Relative Strength Index)
RSI = 100 – [100 / (1 + RS)] where RS = Avg Gain / Avg Loss (14 periods)
Momentum oscillator 0-100. Above 70 = overbought (consider short). Below 30 = oversold (consider long). Best with divergences. Developed by J. Welles Wilder.
Current: 58.4 — NEUTRAL-BULL
MACD (Moving Avg Convergence Divergence)
MACD = EMA(12) – EMA(26) | Signal = EMA(9) of MACD
Trend-following momentum. Signal line crossovers generate buy/sell signals. Histogram = MACD - Signal. Divergences signal reversals. Gerald Appel, 1979.
Current: +0.84 — BULLISH CROSSOVER
Bollinger Bands
Upper = SMA(20) + 2σ | Lower = SMA(20) - 2σ
Volatility bands. Price touching upper band = overbought; lower band = oversold. Band squeeze (low vol) precedes expansion. %B shows position within bands. John Bollinger, 1983.
Current: Price at 67%B — UPPER HALF
Stochastic Oscillator
%K = (C - L14) / (H14 - L14) × 100 | %D = SMA(3) of %K
Momentum comparing close to high-low range. Above 80 = overbought. Below 20 = oversold. %K/%D crossovers and divergences. George Lane. Works best in ranging markets.
Current: %K 62.4 %D 58.1 — BULL CROSS
Volume Profile / VWAP
VWAP = Σ(Price × Volume) / Σ(Volume)
Volume Weighted Average Price. Institutional benchmark. Price above VWAP = bullish; below = bearish. POC (Point of Control) = highest volume node. Key S/R levels.
VWAP: $187.42 — PRICE ABOVE
ATR (Average True Range)
ATR = EMA(14) of max(H-L, |H-Cp|, |L-Cp|)
Measures market volatility. Used to size positions (risk 1-2% per ATR). Set stop-loss distances. Higher ATR = higher volatility = wider stops needed. Wilder, 1978.
ATR(14): $3.84 — MODERATE VOL
Fibonacci Retracements
Levels: 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%, 100%
Based on Fibonacci sequence. Key retracement levels act as support/resistance in trends. 61.8% ("Golden Ratio") is most significant. Used to find entries in pullbacks.
Key Fib Support: $182.14 (61.8%)
Ichimoku Cloud
Tenkan(9), Kijun(26), Senkou A&B, Chikou
Full trading system. Kumo (cloud) provides S/R. Price above cloud = bullish. Tenkan/Kijun cross = signal. Chikou confirms trend. Goichi Hosoda, 1968. Japan origin.
Price ABOVE Cloud — BULLISH
OBV (On-Balance Volume)
OBV = prev OBV + (Vol if close↑) or - (Vol if close↓)
Cumulative volume measure. Rising OBV with rising price = confirms trend. Rising OBV with falling price = bullish divergence (accumulation). Joseph Granville, 1963.
OBV Trend: RISING — CONFIRMS BULL
ADX (Average Directional Index)
ADX = EMA(14) of |DI+ - DI-| / (DI+ + DI-) × 100
Measures trend strength (not direction). Above 25 = strong trend. Below 20 = weak/ranging. Use with +DI/-DI for direction. ADX rising = strengthening trend. Wilder, 1978.
ADX: 32.4 — STRONG UPTREND
Williams %R
%R = (H14 - C) / (H14 - L14) × -100
Momentum oscillator -100 to 0. Above -20 = overbought. Below -80 = oversold. Similar to Stochastic but inverted. Larry Williams. Good for identifying turning points.
%R: -34.2 — NEUTRAL
CCI (Commodity Channel Index)
CCI = (Typical Price - SMA) / (0.015 × Mean Deviation)
Above +100 = strong uptrend / overbought. Below -100 = downtrend / oversold. Zero line crossovers signal direction change. Donald Lambert, 1980. Works in all markets.
CCI: +124.8 — STRONG BULL

VALUATION METRICS COMPLETE REFERENCE
PRICE MULTIPLES
P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)
P/E = Stock Price / EPS
Most common valuation metric. High P/E = growth expectations or overvaluation. S&P 500 historical avg: ~16-17x. Forward P/E uses next-12-month estimates. Cyclical stocks mislead at cycle extremes.
P/S Ratio (Price-to-Sales)
P/S = Market Cap / Annual Revenue
Useful for unprofitable companies. Harder to manipulate than earnings. SaaS companies often valued at 5-20x. Below 1x = possibly undervalued.
EV/EBITDA
EV = Market Cap + Debt - Cash; EV/EBITDA is capital structure neutral
Better than P/E for comparing across capital structures. Neutral to leverage and depreciation differences. Typical ranges: 8-12x value, 15-25x growth.
P/B Ratio (Price-to-Book)
P/B = Market Cap / Shareholders' Equity
Below 1x = trading below book value (asset play). Key metric for banks and financials. High P/B normal for intangible-heavy companies. Graham: buy below 1.5x.
PROFITABILITY METRICS
ROE (Return on Equity)
ROE = Net Income / Shareholders' Equity
Efficiency of equity capital use. Above 15% = strong. Buffett prefers consistently above 20%. Can be inflated by leverage (DuPont analysis: ROE = Net Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier).
ROIC (Return on Invested Capital)
ROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital
Best measure of economic value creation. ROIC > WACC = value created. ROIC < WACC = value destroyed. Superior to ROE as it's leverage-neutral.
Free Cash Flow (FCF)
FCF = Operating Cash Flow - CapEx
Cash available after maintaining/expanding business. Harder to manipulate than earnings. Basis for DCF valuation. FCF Yield = FCF / Market Cap. Target: above 5%.
Gross / Operating / Net Margins
Gross = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue. Operating adds overhead/R&D. Net is bottom line. Software: gross margins 70-90%+. Retail: 25-40%. Banks use different metrics (NIM, efficiency ratio).
GROWTH & QUALITY METRICS
PEG Ratio
PEG = P/E Ratio / Earnings Growth Rate
Adjusts P/E for growth. Below 1.0 = potentially undervalued relative to growth. Above 2.0 = paying dearly for growth. Peter Lynch's preferred metric.
DCF Valuation
V = Σ FCFt / (1+r)^t + Terminal Value / (1+r)^n
Intrinsic value = PV of all future cash flows. Highly sensitive to discount rate (WACC) and terminal growth rate. "Garbage in, garbage out" - use as sanity check not gospel.
Moat Analysis (Competitive Advantage)
Types: Network effects (Meta), Cost advantage (Walmart), Switching costs (Oracle), Intangibles/Brands (Coca-Cola), Efficient scale (utilities). Wider moat = higher justifiable valuation multiple.
Altman Z-Score (Bankruptcy Risk)
Z = 1.2X₁ + 1.4X₂ + 3.3X₃ + 0.6X₄ + X₅
Predicts bankruptcy risk. Z > 2.99 = safe. 1.81-2.99 = grey zone. Below 1.81 = distress. Applies to manufacturing firms primarily. Edward Altman, 1968.

ECONOMIC CONCEPTS REFERENCE
Quantitative Easing (QE)
MONETARY POLICY
Central bank buys assets (bonds) to inject money into economy and lower long-term rates. Expands balance sheet. Stimulative. Opposite: Quantitative Tightening (QT).
Yield Curve Inversion
RECESSION INDICATOR
2Y yield > 10Y yield. Historically precedes recession by 6-24 months. Most reliable predictor: every US recession since 1955 was preceded by inversion. Currently inverted since 2022.
Taylor Rule
INTEREST RATE FRAMEWORK
i = r* + π + 0.5(π - π*) + 0.5(y - y*)
Prescribes optimal fed funds rate. r* = neutral rate, π = inflation, π* = 2% target, y-y* = output gap. Currently suggests rates near current levels.
Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)
FOREX THEORY
Exchange rates should equalize purchasing power across countries. "Big Mac Index" is famous PPP proxy. Long-run predictor of exchange rate direction, not useful for short-term.
Velocity of Money
MONETARY THEORY
V = GDP / M2 Money Supply
Rate at which money circulates. Falling velocity reduces inflationary impact of money creation. COVID-era QE was less inflationary initially due to falling velocity.
Fiscal Multiplier
FISCAL POLICY
How much economic output increases per dollar of government spending. Typically 0.8-1.5x. Higher in recessions (Keynesian), lower during expansion or high debt environments.

🎯 INVESTMENT STRATEGIES — Comprehensive guide to every major strategy used by professionals
EQUITY STRATEGIES
Value Investing
Buy stocks trading below intrinsic value. Margin of safety principle. Deep research on fundamentals. Key metrics: low P/B, P/E, P/FCF. Masters: Buffett, Munger, Graham, Schloss. Long time horizon (5-10+ years).
Growth Investing
Buy companies growing faster than average. Willingness to pay premium P/E for high growth. Focus on revenue growth, TAM, market share. Masters: Lynch, Fisher, Wood. Key metrics: Revenue growth, NRR, LTV/CAC.
GARP (Growth at Reasonable Price)
Hybrid of value and growth. Peter Lynch's approach. PEG ratio below 1.0 ideal. Avoids overpaying for growth while not missing compounders. Most balanced long-term approach.
Dividend Growth Investing
Buy Dividend Aristocrats (25+ years increases) or Kings (50+). Focus on dividend growth rate, payout ratio, coverage ratio. Compounding through reinvestment (DRIP). Examples: JNJ, KO, PG, MMM.
Momentum Investing
Buy relative strength leaders. 12-1 month price momentum. Fama-French documented premium. Rebalance monthly/quarterly. Works until it doesn't—abrupt reversals. Combine with quality factor.
MACRO & ALTERNATIVE STRATEGIES
Global Macro
Top-down analysis of economies, politics, rates, currencies. Trade currencies, rates, indices, commodities. Masters: Soros (reflexivity), Druckenmiller, Ray Dalio (All Weather). High leverage, directional bets.
Trend Following / CTA
Systematic, rules-based trend capture across all asset classes. Long when 200MA rising, short when falling. Managed Futures (CTA strategy). Uncorrelated to equities. Works in tail events. Bridgewater, AQR, Man AHL.
Statistical Arbitrage
Pairs trading, mean reversion of correlated instruments. Long/short pairs that diverge from historical correlation. Quantitative, market-neutral. Renaissance Technologies, DE Shaw. Requires sophisticated models.
Event-Driven
Merger arbitrage, spin-offs, bankruptcies, special situations. Buy target in M&A at discount to deal price. Catalyst-driven returns uncorrelated to market. Kyle Bass, Bill Ackman. Risk: deal break.
Risk Parity
Allocate by risk contribution, not capital. Equalize volatility contributions. Bonds leveraged to equal equity vol. Diversification by risk factor not asset class. Ray Dalio's All Weather Portfolio.
FIXED INCOME & OPTIONS STRATEGIES
Barbell Strategy (Bonds)
Invest in very short AND very long duration bonds, nothing in the middle. Benefits from both yield and potential rate cuts. Nassim Taleb also applies concept to life decisions (safe + moonshots).
The Wheel Strategy (Options)
Step 1: Sell Cash-Secured Put (get paid to potentially buy stock). If assigned, Step 2: Sell Covered Call (get paid to potentially sell stock). Repeat for continuous premium income. Works best on bullish, high-IV stocks.
Poor Man's Covered Call (PMCC)
Buy deep ITM LEAP call (acts as stock proxy) + sell near-term OTM call. Requires much less capital than owning shares. Defined max risk. Synthetic covered call without stock ownership.
Dollar Cost Averaging (DCA)
Invest fixed amount at regular intervals regardless of price. Removes emotion, reduces timing risk. Buys more shares at lower prices naturally. Proven to outperform lump-sum in most bear markets and volatile periods.
Factor Investing (Smart Beta)
Target documented return premiums: Value, Growth, Momentum, Quality, Low Volatility, Size. Academic research: Fama-French, Carhart. RAFI, AQR implement systematic factor tilts. Long-term outperformance with higher drawdowns.
ASSET ALLOCATION FRAMEWORKS
60/40 Portfolio
Classic: 60% equities, 40% bonds. Negative correlation historically between stocks and bonds provides diversification. Has struggled in inflationary periods where both assets decline together.
All Weather Portfolio (Dalio)
30% Stocks, 40% LT Bonds, 15% Intermediate Bonds, 7.5% Gold, 7.5% Commodities. Designed to perform in any economic environment. Very low drawdown, lower returns than 100% equity.
Permanent Portfolio (Browne)
Equal 25% each: Stocks, Bonds, Gold, Cash. Rebalance when any allocation goes outside 15-35%. Extreme simplicity, low volatility. Harry Browne. Each 25% thrives in one economic environment.
Yale Endowment Model
Heavy alternatives: Private Equity, Venture Capital, Real Assets, Hedge Funds. Pioneered by David Swensen. Leverage illiquidity premium. Not replicable by most retail investors without accredited status and capital.
